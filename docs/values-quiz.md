# PathwayAI Values Quiz – Draft Question Set

Nov 20, 2026 · @Maylene

## Design rules

The quiz has 11 questions, takes about 4½ minutes, and every student sees the same questions in the same order.

- **Not leading.** No question changes based on earlier answers. Follow-ups are prewritten, never generated by claude, and only clarify an answer. Open questions come before multiple choice so options don't put words in students' mouths.  
- **Balanced options.** Non-ordered options appear in random order. Each A/B choice has an upside and a cost on both sides. Every question offers "Not sure" or "No preference."  
- **Mostly MECE.** Each attribute has one main question. Values and interests, the most important matching factors, each get a second read.  
- **Illustrative placeholders.** Free-text boxes show a light gray example that disappears when the student types. Each box draws from a rotating pool of about 8 examples across education levels and fields, so no single job is suggested. The question stays visible as a label, and the gray meets WCAG contrast.  
- **Momentum.** Two short text questions up front, then quick taps. One question per screen, with a progress bar.  
- **Rules careers in.** Q9 (avoid) and Q10 (training) shape only the three cards. Careers they screen out still appear in "Where it leads" and browse mode with a neutral label.  
- **Anti-bias.** No income, race, gender or grades. Stage 1 never turns family expectations or prestige into matching tags; a "respected" reason maps only to the Recognition value.

## The questions

Gray text is placeholder. Card factors are numbered as in the Career Card Layers update (F1–F10).

### Q1. Dream job

**"Is there a job you've ever imagined having? What draws you to it?"**

- *Format:* two short text boxes, "Job" and "Because…", with a link below: "I don't have one yet."  
- *Placeholder (rotates):* "Electrician / because I like working with my hands and want to start earning soon." "Game designer / because I love making things other people have fun with."  
- *If "I don't have one yet":* the boxes become **"Describe a workday you'd enjoy, from morning to night."** Placeholder (rotates): "I'd start early, spend most of the day solving problems with a small team, and be done by 5."  
- *Feeds:* values and interests from the "because" (F1, F2); a pay-priority flag; the dream job title, checked against Q10 for the constraint-conflict path (F4).

### Q2. Activities

**"Which of these would you enjoy doing on a free afternoon? Pick up to 3."**

- *Format:* picture cards, random order.  
- *Options:* Fix or build something · Figure out how something works · Run an experiment · Draw, design or make music · Write or record something for others to see · Help someone work through a problem · Teach someone a new skill · Organize a group or event · Start a small business or side hustle · Plan a budget or keep things organized · Take care of an animal or a garden · Cook for people  
- *Feeds:* the 6 O\*NET interest areas, 2 cards each (F1).

### Q3. What people ask you for

**"What's something people tend to ask you for help with?"**

- *Format:* one short text box, optional.  
- *Placeholder (rotates):* "My cousins always ask me to fix their phones." "Friends come to me when they need to talk something out."  
- *Feeds:* interests and strengths, a second read on Q2 (F1, F5).

### Q4. What matters most

**"Which 2 of these matter most to you in a job? Then pick 1 that matters least."**

- *Format:* tap to sort seven cards, random order.  
- *Options:* See the results of my work *(Achievement)* · Decide for myself how to do things *(Independence)* · Get noticed and move up *(Recognition)* · Help people and work with people I like *(Relationships)* · Have a boss and company that support me *(Support)* · Good pay and steady work *(Working Conditions)* · Keep learning new skills and discover things no one knows yet *(Curiosity)*  
- *Feeds:* the main values read (F2). The card wording is reused on "Why it fits you," so students see their own words.  
- *Curiosity note:* this is not one of the six O\*NET Work Values, so matching needs its own dataset field. A candidate is the O\*NET Investigative interest score plus Work Activities such as "Updating and Using Relevant Knowledge" (confirm at download). Q5 has no Curiosity pair; Q2's Investigative cards give the second read.

### Q5. Which job sounds better

**"For each pair, which job sounds better to you?"**

- *Format:* three A/B taps, each with "No preference."  
- *Pairs:*  
  - Your manager gives you a clear plan to follow · You figure out your own way of doing things *(Support vs. Independence)*  
  - Your work gets noticed and you can move up quickly, but it's competitive · Your team shares the credit and everyone backs each other up *(Recognition vs. Relationships)*  
  - Steady, predictable work, even if the tasks feel similar day to day · A chance at big results, even if some projects don't work out *(Working Conditions vs. Achievement)*  
- *Feeds:* a second read on Q4; each value appears once (F2).

### Q6. Work setting

**"Where would you like to spend most of your workday?"**

- *Format:* two sliders, each with a marked middle labeled "No preference."  
  - Indoors ↔ Outdoors  
  - Sitting at a desk ↔ On my feet and moving  
- *Feeds:* work context (F8).

### Q7. Working with the public

**"How much of your day would you like to spend working with customers, patients or the public?"**

- *Format:* single choice: Most of it · Some of it · Very little · Not sure  
- *Feeds:* public contact (F8).

### Q8. Schedule

**"What kind of work schedule sounds best to you?"**

- *Format:* single choice, random order, with "No preference" last: The same days and hours every week · Hours that change week to week, depending on what's needed · Busy during certain seasons, slower the rest of the year · No preference  
- *Then:* **"How many hours a week would you want to work?"** Usually 40 or less · More than 40 some weeks is fine · No preference  
- *Feeds:* schedule regularity and weekly hours (F7). The options mirror O\*NET's Regular, Irregular and Seasonal schedule categories (confirm at download). Collect now; use in matching once the Work Context fields are in the dataset (Beta stretch).

### Q9. Things to avoid

**"Are there kinds of things you'd want to avoid in a job? Check any that apply, then pick the specific ones."**

- *Format:* two steps on one screen.  
  - **Step 1:** checkboxes for five categories, plus "Something else" and "None of these."  
  - **Step 2:** checking a category opens its chips underneath, and the student picks only the conditions they want to avoid. Unchecked categories stay closed. Chips appear in random order within each category.  
- *Categories and chips:*  
  - **Physical:** Heavy lifting · Working at heights · Cramped or awkward spaces · Driving most of the day  
  - **Surroundings:** Blood or medical procedures · Loud noise · Very hot or cold places · Dirt, chemicals or strong smells · Dangerous tools or machines · Being on a computer most of the day  
  - **Schedule:** Night shifts · Weekend or holiday work  
  - **People:** Dealing with upset or angry people · Frequent arguments or conflict · Selling things or meeting sales goals  
  - **Pressure:** Tight deadlines · Being responsible for other people's safety · Work where a mistake could be serious  
- *"Something else" placeholder (rotates):* "Talking on the phone all day." "Working underground."  
- *Feeds:* the `avoid_flags` field (see below). Only the specific chips become flags; checking a category with no chips picked screens nothing out. Shapes the three cards only; screened-out careers stay visible elsewhere with a label (F7, F8). "Sitting most of the day" is left out because Q6 covers it.

### Q10. Training

**"How much more school or training after high school are you open to?"**

- *Helper text under the question:* "On average, jobs that need more training pay more. More training also takes more time and can cost more, and there are good-paying jobs at every level." Followed by a link: [See the data (BLS)](https://www.bls.gov/careeroutlook/2026/data-on-display/education-pays.htm). The text is fixed copy with no numbers, reviewed with the rest of the wording.  
- *Format:* single choice, in order: Start working right away · A few months · About 1–2 years · About 4 years · More than 4 years · Not sure yet  
- *Below it, a checkbox:* "I'd be interested in training where I get paid while I learn (an apprenticeship)."  
- *Feeds:* maximum education and Job Zone ceiling (F4, F5); apprenticeship option (F4).

### Q11. ZIP code

**"What's your ZIP code? (Optional)"**

- *Helper text:* "We use this to show pay, jobs and in-state school costs near you. We don't save it."  
- *Feeds:* metro for wages and demand (F3, F10); state for in-state net price and state aid (F4, guide).

Family income is not in the quiz. It's asked on the results screen, optional, and used only for the net price lookup.

## Follow-ups

A student sees at most 2 follow-ups, chosen in the order below. All are prewritten and only clarify an answer.

| Trigger | Follow-up | Format |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Q1 reason too vague to read values from ("because it's cool") | "What part sounds best to you?" | Chips: The work itself · The people · The pay · Making a difference · Being respected · Other (opens a text box; placeholder: "Getting to be creative every day") · Not sure |
| Q4 skipped, or all three Q5 pairs "No preference" | "If you had to choose, which matters more: what you do every day, or what you get from it (pay, respect, security)?" | A/B \+ Not sure |
| Q10 "Not sure yet" | "Which sounds closer to you right now?" | Start earning sooner · Train longer for more options later · Still not sure |
| Q2 nothing picked | Same cards again: "Pick just 1 that sounds least boring." | Single pick |
| Q9 "Something else" text that doesn't map to any flag | No follow-up | Kept in browser memory for the printed guide only |

If Q10 is still "Not sure," no training ceiling is applied. A Q10 answer of "Start earning sooner" ranks shorter paths higher once the time-to-first-paycheck table exists; it never hides longer paths.

Contradictions between answers (for example, "doctor" in Q1 and "blood or medical procedures" in Q9) get no follow-up during the quiz. They show as a talking point in the counselor guide.

## New dataset field: `avoid_flags`

Q9 needs one new field per occupation in `occupations.json`: a flag for each chip, computed in code from a source field, never by claude.

```json
"avoid_flags": {
  "heights": {
    "value": true,
    "source_field": "O*NET Work Context: Exposed to High Places",
    "score": 3.9,
    "threshold": 3.0,
    "data_vintage": "O*NET 31.0",
    "source_url": "https://www.onetcenter.org/database.html"
  }
}
```

value is true when the score meets the team's threshold, false below it, and null when no source covers it. A null flag never screens a career out and shows a missing-data badge.

- "Something else" text is mapped by Stage 1 to existing flag IDs only and validated against the fixed list. Text that doesn't map is not used in matching.

| Flag ID | Q9 category | Q9 chip | Candidate source (confirm at download) |

## Time budget

The estimate is about 4 minutes 10 seconds for the 11 questions, and just under 5 minutes in the worst case.

- **Text questions (Q1, Q3):** about 1 minute 45 seconds together.  
- **Tap questions (Q2, Q4–Q11):** about 2 minutes 25 seconds. Q9 is the longest at about 25 seconds.  
- **Worst case:** add about 30 seconds for 2 follow-ups and 15 seconds for a Q9 write-in.

If usability testing runs over 5 minutes, cut in this order: Q3 (Q2 already covers interests), then the hours part of Q8.
